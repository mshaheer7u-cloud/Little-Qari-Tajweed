Step 12 · Rule 1

Izhar: say the Noon clearly

نْح

Step 19 · Imala of Raa

Imala: the Raa leans toward "e"

There is only one place in the Quran (Hafs reading) with Imala. In the Mushaf the Raa has a Khari Zer under it. Do not read it as a long "ee": bend the sound toward Kasra, like the "e" in "re" (maj-rē-haa), and stretch it for 2 beats. The Raa is read light.

بِسْمِ اللّٰهِ مَجْرٖىهَا
bismil-laahi maj-rē-haa

Surah Hud, ayah 41. Look for the Khari Zer under the Raa. Read "maj-rē-haa", not "maj-ree-haa".

Step 20 · Ishmam

Ishmam: round your lips, make no sound

Only one word, in Surah Yusuf. It was once two Noons. While saying the merged Noon, round your lips as if saying Pesh, but make no Pesh sound.

Lips round silently on the Noon with Shaddah.

مَا لَكَ لَا تَاْمَنَّا

Step 22 · Tasheel

Tasheel: a soft second Hamza

Only one word, in Surah Fussilat. It starts with two Hamzas. Say the first one clearly, and the second one softly, between Hamza and 'Ayn.

ءَاَعْجَمِيٌّ وَّعَرَبِيٌّ
a-a'jamiyyuw-wa-'arabiyy

Surah Fussilat, ayah 44. Read the second (middle) Hamza softly.

Step 25 · Waqf

Why the right stop matters

Waqf means stopping at the end of a word to take a breath. A stop in the right place keeps the meaning correct. A stop in the wrong place can change it.

Wrong stop
فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّيْنَ

fa-waylul-lil-muṣalleen

Stopping here alone sounds like "So woe to those who pray", which is not the full meaning.

Right way
الَّذِيْنَ هُمْ عَنْ صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُوْنَ

alladheena hum 'an ṣalaatihim saahoon

Read on to the next ayah: "...those who are careless about their prayer." (Al-Ma'un)

3 golden rules

  1. Stop only at the end of a word, never in the middle of a word.
  2. Take your breath only when you stop, not while still reading.
  3. If you had to stop in a wrong place, go back a few words and start again from a good place.
